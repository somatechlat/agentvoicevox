# F3: License and legal implications

## Findings

### [1] EU AI Act Art. 50(2) requires providers of systems that generate synthetic audio (including voice) to mark outputs in a machine-readable format as artificially generated, with effective/interoperable/robust/reliable technical solutions as far as technically feasible.
- quote: "Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs of the AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. Providers shall ensure their technical solutions are effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specificities and limitations of various types of content, the costs of implementation and the generally acknowledged state of the art"
- url: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/
- source_type: primary
- published: Regulation in force 2024-08-01; Art. 50 applies 2026-08-02
- confidence: high

### [2] EU AI Act Art. 50(4) requires deployers of AI that generates or manipulates audio constituting a deepfake to disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated; artistic/creative/satirical works get reduced disclosure but still must reveal existence of generated content.
- quote: "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated. ... Where the content forms part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous work or programme, the transparency obligations set out in this paragraph are limited to disclosure of the existence of such generated or manipulated content in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work."
- url: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/
- source_type: primary
- published: applies 2026-08-02
- confidence: high

### [3] EU AI Act Art. 50(1) also requires that persons interacting with an AI system be informed they are interacting with an AI, unless obvious from context — relevant to AgentVoiceBox voice-agent UX (caller-facing disclosure at first interaction).
- quote: "Providers shall ensure that AI systems intended to interact directly with natural persons are designed and developed in such a way that the natural persons concerned are informed that they are interacting with an AI system ... unless this is obvious from the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed, observant and circumspect"
- url: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/
- source_type: primary
- published: applies 2026-08-02
- confidence: high

### [4] Art. 50 transparency duties come into force 2 August 2026; a Code of Practice on AI-generated content is being developed covering watermarking, metadata, provenance, and modality-specific labelling (e.g. audible disclaimers for audio). Compliance with the Code is expected to be the practical benchmark.
- quote: "What providers and deployers must do by August 2026 ... Technical standards for watermarking, metadata, and provenance tools Guidance on modality-specific labelling (persistent labels for video, visible labels for images, audible disclaimers for audio) Although the Code of Practice is voluntary, it is expected to become the practical benchmark for regulatory assessment."
- url: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-05-14
- confidence: high

### [5] EU AI Act Art. 5 (prohibited practices) bans non-consensual sexually explicit synthetic media and manipulative/subliminal systems — voice cloning that enables sexual deepfakes or exploitation of vulnerable persons is not just "transparency" risk but prohibited; Art. 5 already in force before the 2026 Art. 50 date.
- quote: "intimate parts, or of an identifiable natural person engaged in sexually explicit activities, without that person's freely-given, specific, informed, unambiguous and explicit consent ... for that generation or manipulation"
- url: https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/5/
- source_type: primary
- published: Regulation in force 2024-08-01; amended prohibitions entry 2026-12-02 per page
- confidence: high

### [6] Open-source TTS/voice-clone code licenses are NOT the same as model weight licenses: OpenVoice V1/V2 code+weights are explicitly MIT and free for commercial use, while Coqui XTTS-v2 weights are under Coqui Public Model License (CPML) which allows only non-commercial use — commercial SaaS cannot ship XTTS weights under CPML.
- quote: "OpenVoice V1 and V2 are MIT Licensed. Free for both commercial and research use."
- url: https://github.com/myshell-ai/OpenVoice
- source_type: primary
- published: 2024 (V2 release; MIT)
- confidence: high

### [7] CPML (Coqui Public Model License 1.0.0) is a non-commercial-only model license: it forbids any use where you receive direct or indirect payment from the model or its output, and using the model to train other models for commercial use is expressly not non-commercial.
- quote: "This license allows only non-commercial use of a machine learning model and its outputs. ... Non-commercial purposes include any of the following uses of the model or its output, but only so far as you do not receive any direct or indirect payment arising from the use of the model or its output. ... Use of the model to train other models for commercial use is not a non-commercial purpose."
- url: https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2/raw/main/LICENSE.txt
- source_type: primary
- published: 2023 (Coqui CPML 1.0.0)
- confidence: high

### [8] Coqui TTS codebase itself is MPL-2.0 (permissive for commercial integration) but XTTS model weights carry CPML + tos_required — a commercial SaaS must distinguish code license from weight license; MPL code is fine, CPML weights are not for paid product.
- quote: "Mozilla Public License Version 2.0"
- url: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/coqui-ai/TTS/dev/LICENSE.txt
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (repo current 2026)
- confidence: high

### [9] Voice likeness is a legal right separate from copyright: right of publicity / personality rights cover commercial use of name, image, likeness, and voice; in Canada/EU privacy frameworks, appropriating or using a person's voice is actionable. US states vary (statute vs common law; Tennessee rights may not expire if use is continuous).
- quote: "An appropriation of personality can be achieved through the use of a person's name, likeness, or voice ... The plaintiff must be identified or identifiable by the use made of their persona. ... notably, ... Tennessee's statute provides rights that do not ever expire if use is continuous."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_publicity
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown (wiki, current 2026)
- confidence: medium

### [10] US state deepfake laws create private rights of action without consent (e.g. California AB 602 for sexually explicit deepfakes) and election-related audio deepfake bans (AB 730 within 60 days of election) — commercial voice-cloning SaaS faces state-level liability beyond copyright.
- quote: "Assembly Bill No. 602 provides individuals targeted by sexually explicit deepfake content made without their consent with a cause of action against the content's creator. Assembly Bill No. 730 prohibits the distribution of malicious deepfake audio or visual media targeting a candidate running for public office within 60 days of their election."
- url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfake
- source_type: secondary
- published: unknown (wiki, current 2026)
- confidence: medium

### [11] Commercial SaaS enforcement pattern (ElevenLabs ToS): users may only create voice models for their own voice or "a voice you are authorized to share"; platform takes a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, sublicensable license to the voice for service provision and product improvement, and will not commercialize the voice standalone without permission.
- quote: "Some of our Services allow you to create a voice model that can be used to generate Output in the form of synthetic audio sounding like your voice or a voice you are authorized to share with us (a \"User Voice Model\"). ... we will not commercialize your voice on a standalone basis without your permission to do so. Such license shall be: perpetual and irrevocable (which means this license cannot be withdrawn), nonexclusive ... royalty-free and fully paid ... worldwide ... and sub-licensable, through multiple tiers"
- url: https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (current ToS, fetched 2026-09)
- confidence: high

### [12] Professional voice cloning products require identity verification of the voice owner (not just consent paperwork): ElevenLabs only allows PVC of your own voice, requires a verification process, and blocks cloning others even with their consent — the third party must create/verify in their own account then share via link. This is the practical "opt-in + identity verification" bar for commercial SaaS.
- quote: "No. You can only create a Professional Voice Clone of your own voice. Even with their consent, you cannot clone someone else's voice. All Professional Voice Clones require a verification process to confirm that the voice belongs to you. If someone wants to share their voice with you, they can create and verify a Professional Voice Clone on their own account, then share it with you privately using a sharing link."
- url: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/product-guides/voices/voice-cloning
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (docs, fetched 2026-09)
- confidence: high

### [13] Commercial platforms also implement provenance/traceability and abuse response as legal-risk mitigants: generated audio is traceable to the generating user, infringements are reviewed/actioned, and Prohibited Use Policy bans illegal/harmful use.
- quote: "We seek to support voice owners and their licensors in claiming their rights and all known infringements will be reviewed and actioned; All audio generated by our models can be instantly traced back to the user responsible for the generation."
- url: https://elevenlabs.io/docs/product-guides/voices/voice-cloning
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (docs, fetched 2026-09)
- confidence: high
